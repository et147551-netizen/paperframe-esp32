Pair this browser

This browser is not paired with the frame yet. Pairing needs somebody at the frame, which is the only thing that proves you are allowed in.

  1. Hold the frame's function button for 5 seconds — the top one, marked TOP on the M5Paper Color and GREEN on the reTerminal.
  2. The panel draws a connect card. Wait for it: a refresh takes 15 to 30 seconds.
  3. Type the code from the bottom of that card here.

A phone can scan the two QR codes on that card instead: the first joins the frame's Wi-Fi, the second opens this page already paired.

Settings

Which way up the frame hangs. The two of each shape are the same shape turned the other way round, so pick whichever puts the picture upright for how yours is standing — the angle under each is how far the picture is turned from the screen's own top edge. The button on the frame steps through all four in this order.
The six colours this panel can show are matched against one of several calibrations. No single one suits every picture: the calibrated sets keep shadow detail but can push dark browns towards green, while the uncalibrated set gives the cleanest neutrals and the deepest blacks. Changing this redraws the current photo.
Adjust each photo automatically
Works out what kind of picture each photo is — photograph, faded scan, line art, screenshot, flat artwork — and picks its brightness, contrast and dithering to suit. Off by default, because it also decides for itself whether to dither, overriding the choice made at upload. Changing this redraws the current photo.
Spread dithering error
Carries the rounding error of each pixel into its neighbours in both directions instead of only along the row, which gives smoother gradients and finer texture. Independent of the setting above, so the two can be compared one at a time. It has no effect on photos set to Nearest. Changing this redraws the current photo.
Turn photos to fill the screen
Turns a photo a quarter turn when it is the other way up from the frame, so that it fills the screen instead of sitting in a band across the middle — the difference is roughly half the screen against nearly all of it. Photos that are close to square are left as they were framed. This changes nothing on the card and nothing about the orientation above. Changing this redraws the current photo.
Panel maintenance
The colour check fills the screen with each colour the panel can show, one at a time, with white in between so that each one is seen on its own. It ignores the palette and the dithering entirely, so what you see is the ink itself — if a colour has started to fade, this is where it shows. Ten screens, a few minutes. The frame stays usable while it runs, and the next photo appears afterwards.
Leaves the screen white. Worth doing before switching the frame off or moving it — this kind of screen keeps whatever it was last given, and a picture left on it for a long time can leave a faint trace. It changes no settings; the next photo appears at the usual time.
Flashes the whole screen black and white three times over, ending white. The stronger version of blanking, for a screen that still shows a faint trace of an earlier picture after a blank. Takes a few minutes, and the frame stays usable throughout.
 
Runs after the active hours end, spread over an hour so the panel does not warm up — a warm panel shows washed-out colour, which would look like a fault when it is not. Needs Quiet Hours on, under Slideshow, since that is what sets the quiet window.
Auto Slideshow
Quiet Hours
Outside the active hours below, the frame stops changing the picture and stops fetching new ones. It stays reachable the whole time — this page keeps working, and the next photo appears when the active window reopens. Needs the clock, which the frame sets from the internet once a day; until it has one it refreshes as usual.
Rest on a blank screen
During the quiet hours, show a blank white screen instead of leaving the last photo up all night. This screen keeps whatever it was last given even with the power off, and a picture left on it for hours can leave a faint trace behind — a blank night prevents that, and clears the day's last photo completely so the morning starts fresh. It costs one extra screen refresh a day. Takes effect at the next quiet period, so nothing changes now.
Deep clean at the start of each quiet period
Instead of one blank screen, run the full black-and-white clean when the quiet hours begin. Off by default: this screen has a finite number of refreshes in it, and doing this nightly uses about three times as many as the frame otherwise spends on upkeep. Turn it on if a faint trace of an old picture keeps coming back. On the night the weekly colour check runs, the check is done instead — it already ends on a blank screen.
A battery kept completely full wears out faster than one kept near 80%. This frame normally runs from its power adapter, so stopping short costs you almost nothing and the battery lasts longer. Pick “Charge all the way” before taking the frame somewhere without power. Changing this takes effect immediately.

Shuffle the order
Shows the photos in a shuffled order instead of by filename. Every photo appears exactly once before any repeats, and a new order is drawn when the round finishes. Takes effect from the next photo, so nothing is redrawn now.

Show the whole share
Keeps a rolling cache of about a hundred photos instead of copying one folder, and fetches each photo shortly before it is shown. That is what lets every photo on the share appear, rather than the same few hundred. List several folders in Folder, separated by commas. Photos are removed from the frame as they scroll out of the cache; they stay on the share and come back in their turn.
Shrink photos as they arrive
Saves each photo at the size the screen shows it instead of at its original size, and prepares its thumbnail at the same time. Photos take up much less room on the frame and this page loads far faster, at the cost of a slower sync and a small loss of detail in the stored copy. The photo on the share is never changed. Photos already on the frame are left as they are.
Show photos from Google Photos albums
In Google Photos, open an album, choose Share, then Create link, and paste that link here. An album shared only with particular people cannot be shown. Anyone who has the link can see every photo in the album, so share it only with people you trust. The frame keeps each album's list and fetches each photo shortly before showing it, keeping about a hundred on the frame, and shows photos from all albums together. The albums are checked once a day, or now with Sync now; photos removed from every album are removed from the frame. This uses the slideshow's random order, which is on by default.
